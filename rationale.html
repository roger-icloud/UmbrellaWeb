First a few hard truths:


Number-fun is something I would have wanted when working with youngsters.
Here, I set out to make tables a little easier on the learner (and the teacher/adult).
Rapid recall is a useful skill but there seems to be no shortcut to acquiring it.

What you will find

An attempt to make the learning curve less steep
An opportunity to develop visual skills, fine motor skills and anticipation alongside the main purpose.
Displays which are useful and memorable to encourage learning and understanding.
Insights that deepen understanding of how numbers work.

(We are not replacing Repetition as a powerful learning tool simply complementing it.)

What you won’t find


Illusory rewards, no pretence here…
Time trials or competitiveness…Learners are individuals going at their own pace.
input devices are kept to a absent…you won’t need a keyboard or a mouse….We have attempted to place the material immediately ‘there’ for the learner no need to be distracted by or acquire keyboarding skills to benefit from the material presented here.Language is also kept to a minimum for much the same reason. ….
One picture is more valuable than a lot of words…
Oh and there is no promotion of commercial products or gathering of personal data to sell on.You won’t find any attempt to gain from your visit…(The world had been kind to me…I would like to give something back.)

Repetition is useful in committing facts to memory, repetition with understanding, more so.
